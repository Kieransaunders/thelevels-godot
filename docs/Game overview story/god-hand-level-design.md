# The God Hand: Albion’s Roots

## Concept

A spiritual, elemental puzzle-adventure inspired by *From Dust*, set in post-apocalyptic Somerset after climate collapse and an AI war. Survivors travel between dormant power nodes along fictional ley lines, rebuilding communities and creating a new Druidic faith around a mysterious nature spirit.

The God Hand is real—or appears to be real—but its abilities are strictly limited to manipulating physical elements:

- Water
- Earth
- Stones
- Fire
- Lava
- Wind
- Lightning

The God Hand cannot build, plant, carry, breed or make decisions for the survivors. The tribe must perform those tasks. Their survival, population, cooperation, songs, dances and rituals strengthen the God Hand.

> The God Hand moves the elements. The tribe turns those movements into civilisation.

## Setting

The Somerset Levels have become a shifting inland wetland after sea-level rise, storms, failed flood defences and climate collapse. Drowned villages, raised islands, reed beds, broken roads, flooded churches, abandoned farms and autonomous AI war machines define the landscape.

The survivors have turned to a new Druidic tradition. They raise maypoles, build stone circles, plant sacred groves, sing and dance around ritual sites. Every completed ritual strengthens the nature spirit and activates another point in a growing power network.

The route is inspired by modern ley-line folklore and real sacred landscapes, but the game treats the energy network as fictional. Frome acts as a local feeder route into the larger Albion current, which ultimately connects Somerset, Wiltshire, the Midlands and Scotland.

## Central hub: Avebury

Avebury is the campaign’s central hub and the centre of four or five branching power lines. At the start, its stone landscape is dormant, damaged and partly buried beneath flood sediment and vegetation.

Every completed chapter contributes to Avebury:

- A recovered standing stone.
- A new maypole design.
- A survivor community.
- A ritual song or tradition.
- A machine component.
- A newly awakened elemental power.

The tribe’s stone-bearers return through portals to Avebury with large stones from each region. This provides the game’s fictional explanation for how prehistoric standing stones could have been transported: the God Hand moves the stones through portals, while survivors prepare, guide and raise them at the destination.

As the branches are completed, Avebury transforms:

1. Water begins flowing through the avenues.
2. Buried stones rise and complete new circles.
3. A central ember ignites.
4. Lightning begins connecting the stones.
5. Wind carries ribbons, seeds and songs across the site.
6. Roots spread through the entire monument complex.

When four or five branches are complete, Avebury opens a mega portal to another reality, setting up the next game.

## Campaign structure

Each level follows this loop:

1. Arrive at a local power node.
2. Establish or protect a survivor settlement.
3. Solve environmental problems using available elemental powers.
4. Build structures and prepare a ritual site.
5. Complete a maypole, grove or stone-circle ceremony.
6. Unlock or strengthen an elemental power.
7. Open a portal to the next location.
8. Return a major standing stone and other cultural materials to Avebury.

Portals are not generic magic doors. They are temporary alignment events created by the interaction of the local environment, the ritual site, the tribe and the God Hand.

## Elemental progression

### Water

Water is the first power. It begins weakly and grows through several levels.

- Detect damp ground and underground water.
- Move and drop small quantities.
- Redirect channels.
- Fill or drain sluices.
- Control floods and tides.

The portal begins as a puddle or pool, then grows into a vertical sheet of water and eventually a stable water gate.

### Earth

Earth provides temporary infrastructure.

- Raise causeways.
- Fill channels and pits.
- Stabilise banks.
- Expose buried objects.
- Build ramps and embankments.

Earth paths are quick to build but erode under rain, flooding, wind, machines and population traffic.

### Stones

Stones are distinct from loose earth.

- Lift rocks.
- Roll boulders.
- Stack and rotate stones.
- Build retaining walls.
- Transport large ritual stones through portals.
- Raise standing stones at Avebury.

### Fire

Fire introduces spread, heat and risk.

- Light signal fires.
- Burn invasive vegetation.
- Create firebreaks.
- Heat machinery.
- Clear tunnels and smoke out machines.

### Lava

Lava is a late or special power. It may come from geothermal vents, deep machine boreholes or an altered reality.

- Create permanent raised stone paths.
- Melt or reshape stone.
- Heat water and machinery.
- Block or trap machines.
- Build durable portal frames.

Lava is dangerous and requires earth to contain it and water to cool it.

### Wind

Wind manipulates indirect systems:

- Smoke.
- Clouds.
- Seeds.
- Sails.
- Ash.
- Loose debris.
- Sound and ritual ribbons.

### Lightning

Lightning is rare and hazardous.

- Charge batteries.
- Power old machinery.
- Redirect electrical arcs.
- Disable drones.
- Activate dormant systems.
- Connect ritual stones through conductors.

## Survivor systems

The tribe builds all structures and manages civilisation.

### Construction

Survivors can build:

- Shelters.
- Bridges.
- Farms and gardens.
- Firebreaks.
- Water tanks and pumps.
- Maypoles.
- Stone-circle infrastructure.
- Heat-resistant lava channels.
- Wind and lightning conductors.

The God Hand can make construction possible, but cannot perform it directly.

### Population and breeding

The tribe grows through safe shelter, food, clean water, medical care and protection. Population strengthens the God Hand through labour, ritual participation, shared memory and cultural continuity.

A large but divided or starving population should not automatically produce more power. Cooperation and social health matter as much as numbers.

### Ritual

Survivors sing, dance, weave ribbons, raise stones and maintain groves. Rituals are both spiritual practice and practical coordination. They can:

- Strengthen the God Hand.
- Activate a local power node.
- Stabilise portals.
- Connect settlements.
- Influence machine behaviour.

Every settlement develops its own interpretation of the nature spirit. Some may worship water, roots, stones, fire or machines.

## Opening level: The Raised Way

### Location

The flooded outskirts of Avebury. The tribe begins on a stranded island outside the dormant stone complex. Between the camp and the first maypole node is a flooded basin crossed by an unstable raised earth path.

### Objective

Build a temporary land bridge, reach the First May Pike, complete the first ritual and open the first portal to Glastonbury.

### Starting abilities

- Weak earth manipulation.
- Very weak water manipulation.
- Ability to move small stones.
- A small population of survivors.
- Basic boats, ropes and salvaged tools.

### Storm pressure

A violent storm arrives every five minutes. Each storm:

- Erodes the raised earth path.
- Raises the water level.
- Floods low camps.
- Moves loose stones and timber.
- Activates nearby drones.
- Forces the tribe to repair, evacuate or progress.

The storm is predictable, giving the player time to plan, but increasingly punishing if ignored.

### Phase 1: Establish the camp

The tribe creates a safe base on high ground. The player uses weak earth power to raise small banks, fill channels and create ramps. Survivors gather materials and build shelters, storage and repair stations.

### Phase 2: Build the temporary land bridge

Materials:

- Clay and mud.
- Gravel from a collapsed road.
- Timber from drowned buildings.
- Reeds and willow branches.
- Rope and metal from wreckage.

Construction sequence:

1. Place heavy stones as a foundation.
2. Fill gaps with gravel.
3. Pack clay over the surface.
4. Reinforce edges with timber.
5. Weave willow branches into the sides.
6. Use earth power to raise weak sections.

The path reaches toward the maypole but remains vulnerable to every storm.

### Phase 3: Discover the willow

A survivor finds a forked willow branch washed against a flooded hedge. When held over the ground, it bends toward hidden water.

The player uses the branch to search for:

- A genuine spring.
- False surface runoff.
- Leaking pipes.
- Contaminated water.
- Water trapped beneath machines.

The correct spring is identified by willow movement, healthier vegetation, insects and subtle environmental clues. Survivors dig while the God Hand raises the soil.

This introduces dowsing as a practical survivor discovery and as the first interface between human observation and the water power.

### Phase 4: Expose the lava flow

The spring lies beside a dormant geothermal vent or buried lava channel. The tribe must expose it without releasing too much lava.

Tasks:

- Dig a controlled channel.
- Build earth walls.
- Place stones as barriers.
- Keep survivors away from the heat.
- Clear a route toward the maypole.

The lava initially flows beyond the God Hand’s control and creates a dangerous new obstacle.

### Phase 5: Water meets lava

The tribe must cool the lava in stages to create a crossing.

The player:

1. Opens a narrow lava flow.
2. Contains it with earth and stones.
3. Lifts water from the spring.
4. Drops controlled amounts onto the lava.
5. Allows each section to cool into rough black stone.
6. Has survivors reinforce the cooled surface.
7. Repeats the process until a raised crossing is complete.

Too much water causes steam explosions, cracked rock or destructive flooding. Too little leaves the route molten and impassable.

This teaches the relationship between the elements:

> Water controls lava. Lava creates the permanent route. Survivors make that route safe.

### Phase 6: Reach the First May Pike

The maypole stands inside the first accessible part of the Avebury stone complex. It is constructed from:

- A living willow or ash trunk.
- Copper cable.
- Cloth ribbons.
- Machine antenna parts.
- Stone weights.
- Charred timber.

The player must ensure that:

- Enough survivors reach the site.
- The spring remains accessible.
- The crossing connects the camp to the node.
- The maypole is upright.
- The ritual circle is complete.

### Ritual

The player controls elemental conditions while the survivors sing and dance:

- Keep the maypole upright in the storm.
- Guide water through channels.
- Seal earth cracks.
- Maintain the cooled crossing.
- Position stones around the ritual circle.
- Protect dancers from drones.

As the ritual grows stronger, the maypole ribbons rotate and the water network beneath Avebury responds.

### Reward: lava power

The ritual unlocks lava manipulation. The God Hand now directs lava into the temporary earth route, while water from the spring cools it into a permanent raised black-stone path.

The path survives the next storm and becomes the first durable road in the campaign.

The tribe names it:

- The Black Way.
- The Fired Road.
- The Dragon Path.
- The First Stone Road.

### First portal: Avebury to Glastonbury

The completed route and ritual combine to open the first portal.

Visual sequence:

1. Water rises from the spring.
2. Lava glows below the new black-stone path.
3. Steam forms a circular curtain.
4. The maypole ribbons twist into a spiral.
5. The stones lift slightly.
6. A molten, water-edged arch opens.
7. Most survivors cross toward Glastonbury.
8. Stone-bearers return through the portal to Avebury.
9. A newly recovered standing stone is raised in the dormant central circle.

The camera pulls back to show a glowing line between Avebury and Glastonbury.

## Subsequent level ideas

### Glastonbury: The Water Memory

The tribe reaches a flooded Glastonbury and climbs toward the Tor. Water power grows through springs, wells and channels. The player restores a water source and establishes the first major regional ritual.

### Burrow Mump: The Tidal Shrine

A settlement on the mound must survive a storm surge. The player controls floodgates and tide timing, then opens a portal during a brief high-water window.

### Stanton Drew: The Three Circles

The player coordinates water, earth and stones across several prehistoric circles. A new outer circle is built around the old stones, showing that the survivors are creating rather than merely imitating the past.

### Mendip: The Deep Spring

Caves, quarries and underground rivers introduce difficult water and stone puzzles. The God Hand’s influence becomes unreliable below ground until a cave ritual is completed.

### Avebury return

The western water branch is complete. The survivors return a major standing stone to Avebury. The first branch of the central network ignites.

## Branch structure from Avebury

Avebury eventually becomes the centre of four or five branches.

### Western water branch

Frome, Doulting, Burrow Mump, Glastonbury and the Somerset Levels. Focus: water, springs, floods, tides and memory.

### Earth and stone branch

Mendips, Stanton Drew, Cheddar and surrounding uplands. Focus: clay, caves, weight, construction and standing stones.

### Fire branch

Avebury, Silbury Hill, West Kennet, Salisbury Plain and Stonehenge. Focus: heat, solar power, controlled burns, weapons and destruction.

### Northern storm branch

Cotswolds, Malvern Hills, the Lake District, Kilmartin Glen and Scotland. Focus: wind, lightning, weather systems and machine consciousness.

### Hidden root branch

An underground or inter-reality route through aquifers, caves, cables, machine memories and the nature spirit itself. This branch appears only after the visible branches are complete.

## Portal progression

### Water gate

An unstable pool or vertical sheet of water. It transports small groups and objects.

### Earth gate

An earthen arch anchored by a mound. It can transport building materials and supplies.

### Stone gate

A standing-stone doorway with orbiting pebbles. It can transport large ritual stones back to Avebury.

### Fire gate

A heated stone frame surrounded by flame and smoke. It attracts machines and requires defence.

### Storm gate

Wind and lightning connect the portal to distant nodes. The network becomes visible across the landscape.

### Mega portal

When four or five Avebury branches are complete, the whole stone complex becomes a reality engine. Water, earth, stone, fire, lava, wind and lightning combine around a growing root network.

## Mega portal ending

The final sequence of the first game:

1. Each completed branch ignites across Britain.
2. Water rises through Avebury’s avenues.
3. The recovered standing stones rise into position.
4. Fire burns at the centre.
5. Lightning connects the stones.
6. Wind carries the tribe’s song around the circle.
7. Roots spread from the central ritual site.
8. The entire Avebury network opens.
9. The portal fractures into several windows showing different realities.

Possible sequel realities:

- A drowned world of floating settlements.
- A green world ruled by vast plant intelligence.
- An ember world of permanent heat and fire.
- A machine world where the war never ended.
- A sky world of airborne communities and violent weather.
- The internal memory-world of the God Hand.

The first game ends with the tribe stepping toward the unknown reality while Avebury’s completed standing-stone network glows behind them.

## Core design principle

Each new element should solve three kinds of problem:

1. A practical survival problem.
2. A strategic environmental or machine problem.
3. The ritual problem that opens the next portal.

The progression is:

> Water finds the route. Earth raises it. Stone anchors it. Fire gives it energy. Lava makes it permanent. Wind carries the song. Lightning awakens the network. The tribe gives the God Hand purpose.
