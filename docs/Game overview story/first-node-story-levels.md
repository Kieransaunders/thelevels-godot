# The First Node: The Raised Way

## Level arc

**Campaign position:** Prologue / first playable node  
**Primary location:** The flooded outskirts of Avebury  
**Destination:** Glastonbury  
**Primary element:** Earth  
**Secondary discovery:** Water sensing through a willow fork  
**Portal reward:** First portal activation  
**Major contribution:** The first standing stone returned to Avebury  
**Tone:** Simple, hopeful and physical before the wider dangers of the world are revealed

## Story purpose

The tribe begins on a small dry island outside the drowned Avebury stone landscape. They can see the first maypole node across a shallow flood basin, but the old route has collapsed.

The God Hand is almost entirely dormant. It can only move small amounts of earth and loose stone. The tribe must build a very simple land bridge by gathering materials, placing them and using the God Hand to shape the ground.

The level teaches the central relationship of the game:

> The God Hand can move the world, but the tribe must decide what to build.

When the bridge reaches the first maypole, the survivors perform a small ritual. The ritual opens the first portal to Glastonbury and awakens the first stronger aspect of the God Hand.

## Opening situation

The tribe has escaped a machine raid and reached a raised island near Avebury. They have:

- A small group of adult survivors.
- Several children and elderly people.
- A few hand tools.
- A damaged cart.
- Food for only a short time.
- Two small boats.
- A rolled length of rope.
- No permanent shelter.

The island is safe from the current water level but cannot support the tribe for long. The nearest dry route ends at a broken stone causeway leading toward the first maypole node.

The survivors can see the maypole through morning mist. Its ribbons are still, but a faint green glow pulses at its base.

## The first God Hand abilities

The player begins with deliberately limited powers.

### Weak earth movement

The God Hand can:

- Lift a small amount of mud.
- Drop soil into a shallow gap.
- Push loose earth along the ground.
- Raise a small mound.
- Smooth a short section of path.

### Loose stone movement

The God Hand can:

- Lift pebbles.
- Roll small rocks.
- Place stones into gaps.
- Move a few foundation stones.

### Water: present but too weak to matter

The God Hand uses a single auto-selecting hand for earth and water. It takes earth over dry
ground and water over open water, chooses between them by what is under the brush, and
shows which through the cursor ring colour (amber for earth, blue for water). There is no
separate earth tool and water tool to unlock between.

Water is therefore not locked at the start; it is capped. The hand can lift a splash of
standing water and set it down again, which is enough to notice and not enough to change
anything. It cannot stop a current, drain a gap or hold back the flood. The player observes
the channels and builds around them because the hand is too weak to do otherwise, not
because the ability is missing.

The willow branch reveals where water sits below ground. The maypole ritual deepens the
grip enough to raise the portal sheet.

## Level layout

The level is compact and readable, with five connected areas.

### 1. Survivor island

The starting area contains:

- A damaged camp.
- A collapsed farmhouse or barn.
- Timber that can be salvaged.
- A small supply of food.
- A high point for observing the basin.

### 2. Flood basin

A shallow stretch of water separates the island from the old causeway.

Features:

- Mud banks.
- Small channels.
- Loose stones.
- Reeds.
- Broken fencing.
- A few submerged obstacles.

The water is not yet dangerous, but deep sections cannot be crossed on foot.

### 3. Broken causeway

The old road is raised above the basin but has several missing sections. It is the natural route to the maypole.

### 4. First maypole node

The maypole stands within a small ring of half-buried stones. It is the ritual destination and the location where the portal will open.

### 5. Avebury stone field

Several large stones are visible in the distance. They are too heavy to move during this first level, but one smaller standing stone has fallen near the route. It will become the first stone returned through the portal.

## Primary objective

Build a safe land bridge from the survivor island to the First May Pike and complete the first ritual.

## Secondary objectives

Optional objectives reward careful play without blocking the first portal:

- Rescue a survivor stranded on a small mudbank.
- Recover the tribe’s damaged bell.
- Salvage enough timber to improve the bridge.
- Preserve the fallen standing stone from further damage.
- Keep all families together during the crossing.

## Tutorial progression

### Step 1: Secure the island

The player learns to read the hand’s auto-selection — amber ring over dry ground takes
earth, blue ring over water takes water — and to move small amounts of soil. The hand
carries one kind of matter at a time, so scooping water discards carried earth.

Tasks:

- Raise a dry platform for a campfire.
- Fill a flooded depression.
- Create a short ramp from the damaged barn.
- Move stones away from the sleeping area.

The survivors automatically gather materials once the player marks a construction area.

### Step 2: Inspect the old route

A scout reaches the edge of the basin and reports that the old causeway is broken in three places.

The player learns to inspect terrain:

- Blue indicates waterlogged ground.
- Brown indicates loose soil.
- Grey indicates stable stone.
- Red indicates dangerous depth or unstable ground.

The player places route markers, and survivors begin carrying timber, stones and earth toward the first gap.

### Step 3: Build the first section

The first gap is small and simple. The player places stones into the water, then drops earth between them.

Survivors compact the fill and lay timber across the top.

This establishes the basic construction loop:

1. Mark a target.
2. Move material with the God Hand.
3. Let survivors build and reinforce.
4. Test the route.

### Step 4: Solve the second section

The second gap is wider and has a moving channel running through it. If the player fills the whole channel, water backs up and floods the camp.

The correct solution is to:

- Leave a narrow channel open.
- Build a low stone culvert.
- Pack earth around it.
- Lay timber over the top.

The God Hand is not powerful enough to stop the water, so the player must work with its flow.

### Step 5: Solve the third section

The final gap is close to the maypole and contains a fallen stone. The stone is too large to lift, but the player can roll it into position as part of the bridge foundation.

Survivors use ropes and a timber frame to guide it. The God Hand helps by:

- Raising the ground beneath one side.
- Removing mud from its path.
- Slowing its roll with placed stones.

The player learns that large objects require preparation rather than direct control.

### Step 6: Open the route

When the bridge is complete, survivors test it with:

- One scout.
- A cart.
- Food stores.
- Children and elderly people.
- Finally, the whole tribe.

The bridge bends and settles but holds. A final gap remains between the causeway and the maypole circle.

## The willow discovery

Near the final gap, a survivor finds a forked willow branch caught in the reeds. The branch is fresh enough to root if planted.

An older survivor remembers that willow grows where water is near the surface. They hold the branch over the ground, and it bends toward a shallow underground stream.

This is the first suggestion that the tribe can learn to work with the hidden behaviour of the land.

The player uses the willow to identify a safe location for the final bridge support. The God Hand then raises earth beneath it without blocking the underground flow.

The willow is planted beside the completed bridge. It becomes the first sacred tree of the new tradition.

## The First May Pike

The maypole is a damaged communications mast wrapped in cloth, willow and copper wire. The survivors have made it into a ritual structure but have not yet awakened it.

The node contains:

- A central pole.
- Five empty ribbon hooks representing future branches.
- A ring of half-buried stones.
- A shallow channel leading toward the route.
- A broken bell.
- A small dormant machine core at its base.

The pole is called the **First May Pike** because it is both a marker and a spear planted into the land.

## Ritual preparation

The player must prepare the site before the ceremony can begin.

Tasks:

- Clear debris from the stone ring.
- Raise three small stones into position.
- Repair the bell using salvaged metal.
- Plant willow cuttings beside the pole.
- Carry a bowl of clean water from the island.
- Move the fallen stone into the outer edge of the circle.
- Bring every survivor safely across the bridge.

The ritual cannot start if the tribe is divided across the basin or if the maypole is damaged.

## First ritual

The ritual is simple and intimate rather than spectacular.

The survivors form a circle and perform a repeated movement:

1. Walk clockwise.
2. Touch the maypole ribbons.
3. Place a pebble at the base.
4. Turn toward Avebury.
5. Sing the first refrain.
6. Repeat as more survivors join.

The player controls the environment while the ritual continues:

- Keep loose earth from collapsing.
- Move small stones back into the circle.
- Prevent water from cutting across the site.
- Protect the flame used to light the maypole.
- Deflect a scouting drone with a raised earth bank.

The God Hand is not directly commanded by the song. The song gives the God Hand enough strength to respond to the tribe’s collective intention.

## First miracle

At the climax:

- The broken bell rings without being struck.
- The willow cuttings bend toward the maypole.
- Water begins flowing through the shallow channels.
- The three stones rise into upright positions.
- The fallen stone shifts into the outer ring.
- A green line of light travels across the ground toward Avebury.

The God Hand gains its first real water ability: it can lift and drop a small amount of water.

This is not yet enough to control the Levels. It is enough to fill the portal basin.

## First portal: The Water Gate

The portal opens between two small standing stones.

Creation sequence:

1. The player lifts water from the channel.
2. The water forms a narrow vertical ribbon.
3. Survivors continue the song and dance.
4. The God Hand widens the ribbon.
5. The maypole’s ribbons twist into a circular pattern.
6. The water becomes a transparent doorway.
7. Glastonbury Tor appears through the surface.

The portal is unstable and remains open only briefly.

### Portal limitations

- Only people and lightweight supplies can pass.
- Large stones cannot yet be transported.
- The crossing requires continuous ritual singing.
- The portal collapses if the water source is blocked.

At this stage, the first fallen stone is not transported. It is marked and left beside the node, establishing the later idea that completed levels will return major stones to Avebury once the portals become stronger.

## End-of-level cinematic

The tribe crosses the Water Gate toward Glastonbury. The camera remains behind for a moment.

The first maypole glows softly. The willow cuttings take root. The land bridge remains intact. Across the flooded landscape, a thin line of green light connects the node to Avebury.

One survivor remains behind to tend the maypole and the new willow. They place a pebble at its base and begin the song again.

The camera pulls back to reveal that one of Avebury’s buried stones has begun to emerge from the ground.

## Gameplay lessons

The first level teaches:

- The God Hand is limited.
- Survivors build structures.
- Material placement matters.
- Earth can be shaped but not made permanent.
- The hand chooses earth or water by what lies under it; the ring colour says which.
- Water must be worked around before the grip is strong enough to control it.
- A willow branch reveals hidden water.
- Ritual strengthens the God Hand.
- A portal is created through environmental preparation and collective action.
- Avebury is the campaign’s central destination and record of progress.

## Failure conditions

The level should rarely produce a hard game over. Failure creates setbacks instead.

Possible consequences:

- A storm destroys part of the bridge.
- The camp floods and supplies are lost.
- A survivor is injured and cannot work temporarily.
- The ritual is delayed until the site is repaired.
- A drone damages the maypole.
- The player runs out of timber and must salvage more.

A full failure occurs only if:

- The entire camp is submerged.
- The maypole is destroyed.
- No food remains and the tribe cannot reach the node.

## Completion rewards

- First Water Gate.
- Weak water manipulation.
- First maypole node.
- First sacred willow.
- First illuminated ley connection.
- New Glastonbury destination.
- Avebury begins awakening.

## Design summary

The first level should be deliberately small. It is not about saving Somerset or defeating the AI war machines. It is about one group of survivors building one simple bridge to one maypole.

The bridge begins as earth, timber and stone. The portal begins as a little water. The miracle is not that the God Hand solves everything; it is that the tribe learns how to cooperate with it.

> First they build a way across the water. Then the water opens the way to the world.
